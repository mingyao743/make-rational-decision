# Six Thinking Hats Analysis

Use this method to examine a problem or decision from six distinct perspectives, ensuring comprehensive coverage and avoiding single-angle blind spots.

## When to Use

- Team discussions that tend to default to one mode of thinking
- Complex decisions needing balanced evaluation
- Breaking deadlocks where participants talk past each other
- Any situation where a single perspective has dominated the conversation

## The Six Hats

| Hat | Color | Role | Key Question |
|-----|-------|------|--------------|
| White | ⬜ | Facts & Data | What information do we have? What's missing? |
| Red | 🔴 | Emotions & Intuition | How do we feel about this? What's the gut reaction? |
| Black | ⚫ | Risks & Caution | What could go wrong? What are the dangers? |
| Yellow | 🟡 | Value & Optimism | What are the benefits? Why would this work? |
| Green | 🟢 | Creativity & Alternatives | What are alternative approaches? What new ideas emerge? |
| Blue | 🔵 | Control & Process | How should we organize our thinking? What's the next step? |

## Process

1. **Blue Hat — Set the agenda** (always start here)
   - Define the problem or decision to be analyzed
   - Determine the sequence of hats to use (see suggested sequences below)
   - Set time limits for each hat session

2. **White Hat — Gather facts**
   - List all known data and information
   - Identify information gaps
   - Distinguish verified facts from assumptions

3. **Red Hat — Express feelings**
   - Allow each participant to share gut reactions without justification
   - Capture emotional responses: excitement, fear, unease, enthusiasm
   - No debate or challenge allowed during this phase

4. **Black Hat — Assess risks**
   - Identify potential problems, dangers, and obstacles
   - Evaluate what could go wrong with each option
   - Challenge feasibility and identify weaknesses

5. **Yellow Hat — Explore benefits**
   - Identify positive outcomes and value opportunities
   - Explain why the approach could succeed
   - Look for best-case scenarios and upsides

6. **Green Hat — Generate alternatives**
   - Propose creative solutions and workarounds
   - Build on ideas from other hats
   - Encourage unconventional thinking

7. **Blue Hat — Summarize and conclude** (always end here)
   - Synthesize insights from all hats
   - Identify decisions made and actions to take
   - Define next steps

## Suggested Sequences

- **Decision-making**: Blue → White → Green → Yellow → Black → Red → Blue
- **Problem-solving**: Blue → White → Black → Green → Blue
- **Creative exploration**: Blue → White → Green → Yellow → Black → Blue
- **Quick assessment**: Blue → White → Red → Blue

## Output Format

Present results as a structured table or document:

```
## Six Thinking Hats Analysis: [Topic]

### ⬜ White Hat — Facts
- [List of known facts and data points]
- [Information gaps identified]

### 🔴 Red Hat — Emotions
- [Gut reactions and feelings]

### ⚫ Black Hat — Risks
- [Potential dangers and obstacles]

### 🟡 Yellow Hat — Benefits
- [Positive outcomes and value]

### 🟢 Green Hat — Creativity
- [Alternative ideas and approaches]

### 🔵 Blue Hat — Conclusion
- [Synthesized insights]
- [Decisions and next steps]
```

## Rules

- Everyone wears the same hat at the same time — no mixing perspectives mid-session
- The Blue Hat manages the process; it does not contribute content except for synthesis
- Red Hat responses must not be challenged or questioned
- Black Hat focuses on logical risks, not emotional negativity (that's Red Hat's job)
- Time-box each hat session to maintain momentum
