# PEST Analysis

A macro-environmental scanning framework that examines four external factor categories: **Political, Economic, Social, and Technological**.

## When to Use

- Market entry or expansion decisions
- Strategic planning and environmental scanning
- Industry analysis and competitive landscape assessment
- Risk assessment for new investments
- Long-term forecasting and scenario planning

## The Four Dimensions

### P — Political Factors
Government actions and regulatory environment that affect the subject.

- Regulations and compliance requirements
- Trade policies, tariffs, and sanctions
- Political stability and government transitions
- Tax policies and incentives
- Labor laws and employment regulations
- Industry-specific legislation

### E — Economic Factors
Macroeconomic conditions that influence costs, revenues, and market dynamics.

- GDP growth and economic cycles
- Interest rates and inflation
- Exchange rates and currency stability
- Consumer spending power and unemployment
- Cost of capital and investment climate
- Supply chain economics

### S — Social Factors
Demographic and cultural trends that shape demand and workforce.

- Population demographics and growth rates
- Lifestyle trends and consumer behavior shifts
- Cultural attitudes and social norms
- Education levels and workforce skills
- Health and wellness trends
- Public opinion and media sentiment

### T — Technological Factors
Technology developments that create opportunities or disrupt existing models.

- Emerging technologies and innovation cycles
- Automation and digital transformation trends
- R&D investment levels (public and private)
- Technology infrastructure maturity
- Cybersecurity and data privacy technology
- Disruptive innovations on the horizon

## Process

1. **Define the scope and timeframe**
   - What subject are you analyzing? (company, product, market entry)
   - What timeframe? (1 year, 3 years, 5 years)

2. **Scan each dimension** (research-based, not brainstorming)
   - Gather data from industry reports, news, government sources
   - List observable trends, events, and conditions
   - Focus on factors that are **changing** or likely to change

3. **Assess impact and likelihood**
   - For each factor: What is the potential impact (high/medium/low)?
   - What is the probability of occurrence?
   - Is the effect positive (opportunity) or negative (threat)?

4. **Prioritize**
   - Highlight the top 3-5 factors with highest impact × likelihood
   - These become key inputs for strategic planning

5. **Connect to action**
   - How should the strategy adapt to these factors?
   - What monitoring is needed for high-uncertainty factors?

## Extensions

- **PESTEL**: Adds Environmental (carbon regulations, sustainability) and Legal (intellectual property, consumer protection) as separate dimensions
- **STEEPLE**: Adds Environmental, Legal, and Ethical dimensions

## Rules

- PEST analyzes **external** factors only — internal capabilities belong in SWOT
- Each factor should describe a **trend or change**, not a static condition
- Evidence and data sources should be cited where possible
- Avoid speculation — distinguish between observed trends and predictions
- Regularly revisit as macro conditions change
